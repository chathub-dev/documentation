Every video model in ChatHub generates **1 video per request**, supports **4–15 seconds**, and produces **synchronized AI audio** automatically.

## Model Comparison

| Model | Resolutions | Duration | Reference images | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **MiniMax H3** _(default)_ | `768p`, `2k` | 4–15s | Up to 9 | High-res `2k` output, multi-reference scenes (5–9 images) |
| **Seedance 2.5** | `480p`, `720p`, `1080p` | 4–15s | Up to 4 | Cinematic camera motion, strong prompt adherence, quick `480p` drafts |
| **Seedance 2.0** | `480p`, `720p`, `1080p` | 4–15s | Up to 9 | Lower credit cost per second, multi-reference scenes (up to 9 images) |

<Tip>
  **Save credits while iterating:** Draft your idea with a **5-second** clip at a lower resolution (`480p`, `720p`, or `768p`) first. Once composition and motion look right, switch to `1080p` or `2k` and extend the duration for your final render.
</Tip>

## A Reliable Prompt Formula

A clear, beginner-friendly structure is:

> **Subject + Action + Setting & Lighting + Camera Movement + Audio / Atmosphere**

Each part helps the model make concrete visual decisions:

- **Subject:** Who or what is the focus (appearance, clothing, expression, texture).
- **Action:** What visibly changes over the clip — keep it focused on 1–2 continuous actions for a 5-second video.
- **Setting & Lighting:** Time of day, environment, color mood (e.g., _warm golden morning sunbeams_, _rainy neon alley at dusk_).
- **Camera Movement:** Simple cinematic verbs work best — _slow dolly-in_, _gentle pan left_, _smooth tracking shot from behind_, _static locked-off frame_.
- **Audio / Atmosphere:** Since audio is generated automatically, mentioning ambient sound or effects (e.g., _soft pouring water_, _crunching footsteps_, _warm café murmur_) helps keep sound aligned with the visuals.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Cramming too many cuts into 5 seconds:** A single continuous shot almost always looks smoother than describing three different scenes in one short clip.
- **Describing only a still picture:** Adding explicit motion (what moves, how fast, and how the camera reacts) prevents stiff, nearly frozen videos.
- **Re-describing static frames in Image to video:** Let the uploaded First/Last frame define appearance; use your prompt to direct the motion and transition.
- **Vague references in Reference to video:** Use the interactive `Image 1`, `Image 2` chips directly in sentences so the model knows which image controls the character, background, or prop.
