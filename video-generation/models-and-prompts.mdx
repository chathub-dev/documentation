Every video model in ChatHub generates **1 video per request**, supports **4–15 seconds**, and includes **synchronized AI audio** automatically.

## Model Comparison

| Model | Resolutions | Duration | Reference images | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **MiniMax H3** _(default)_ | `768p`, `2k` | 4–15s | Up to 9 | High-resolution `2k` output, multi-reference scenes (5–9 images) |
| **Seedance 2.5** | `480p`, `720p`, `1080p` | 4–15s | Up to 4 | Cinematic camera motion, strong prompt adherence, quick `480p` previews |
| **Seedance 2.0** | `480p`, `720p`, `1080p` | 4–15s | Up to 9 | Lower credit cost per second, multi-reference scenes (up to 9 images) |

<Tip>
  **Save credits while iterating:** Test your composition and motion with a **5-second** clip at a lower resolution (`480p`, `720p`, or `768p`) first. Once the result looks right, switch to `1080p` or `2k` and extend the duration for your final version.
</Tip>

## A Reliable Prompt Framework

A clear structure for consistently strong results is:

> **Subject + Action + Setting & Lighting + Camera Movement + Audio / Atmosphere**

Each element gives the model concrete creative direction:

- **Subject:** Who or what is the visual focus (appearance, clothing, expression, materials).
- **Action:** What visibly changes during the clip — focusing on 1–2 continuous actions produces the smoothest results in a 5-second video.
- **Setting & Lighting:** Time of day, environment, and color mood (for example, _warm golden morning sunbeams_ or _rainy neon street at dusk_).
- **Camera Movement:** Simple cinematic directions work best — _slow dolly-in_, _gentle pan left_, _smooth tracking shot from behind_, or _steady locked-off frame_.
- **Audio / Atmosphere:** Because audio is generated automatically, describing ambient sound or effects (such as _soft pouring water_, _footsteps on gravel_, or _warm café ambience_) helps keep sound aligned with the visuals.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Combining too many scene changes into 5 seconds:** A single continuous shot almost always looks smoother and more coherent than describing multiple scene cuts in one short clip.
- **Describing only a still image:** Adding explicit motion (what moves, at what pace, and how the camera responds) prevents stiff or nearly static videos.
- **Repeating static frame details in Image to video:** Let your uploaded First/Last frame define appearance, and dedicate your prompt to movement and transitions.
- **Leaving image roles ambiguous in Reference to video:** Insert the clickable `Image 1`, `Image 2` tags directly inside your sentences so each image's role is unmistakable.
